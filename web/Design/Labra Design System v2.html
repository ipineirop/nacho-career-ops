The system.

One palette, two modes, three typefaces, ten rules. Every component on every screen of LeadMe lives inside this book. If something isn't here, it isn't in the product yet.

01Color

One accent. Petrol on light, sky-blue on dark — same role, mode-mapped. One signal. Burnt amber on light, saffron on dark, used only for under-market and stale-state warnings. Pulled deliberately away from safety-orange, toward saffron / brass.

15The ten rules

Restrictions are the brand. If the system can't say it, the design is wrong before the system is.

01 One accent. One signal. Petrol/Sky for action. Amber for warning. That's the entire chromatic palette. Third color = wrong design.
02 Two radii. Forever. 10px for cards, score chips, modals, salary bands. 999px for pills, buttons, people avatars. Nothing else exists.
03 No shadows. Ever. Hierarchy comes from borders, radius, density — never blur. A dropped shadow is the design failing.
04 One spacing scale. 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 px. Nothing in between. Ever.
05 Avatars are squares. Companies: 10–12px radius squares, accent-soft, two-letter initials. People: 999px circles. Never photos. Never logos in MVP.
06 Numbers wear a uniform. All scores, percentages, currency, dates render in Geist Mono. Labels uppercase. The "receipts" feel comes from this consistency.
07 Serif is a moment. Fraunces appears at exactly four sizes: 22 (verdict), 28 (brief pick), 44 (brief hero), 68 (doc hero). Never paragraphs.
08 Density follows surface. Brief / Evaluate breathe — 22–32px padding. Tracker tightens — 10–14px row padding. Editorial where it reads, tabular where it operates.
09 Score chips are iconic. Always 56×56 (verdict) or 44×44 (list). Always 10–14px radius. Never a circle. Never anywhere else.
10 No icons until earned. Words over glyphs. If a label needs an icon to be understood, the label is wrong. Iconography only after every screen ships text-first.

16Voice

The product is a candid, calm advisor — never desperate, never breathless. It speaks like someone who's read your CV and respects your time. v2: both languages produced together. Editorial registers diverge in one structural place — em-dashes are an EN-only device.

EN · Do
"Kavak just opened your seat."
"Worth your time — with caveats."

'Competitive' is doing a lot of work in that DM. Market for Head of Ops at a Series C MX fintech with your scope: $1.9–2.4M MXN + 0.1–0.25% equity.

ToneCandid, dry, informed. Speaks to a Director, not at a user.
DictionShort clauses. Em-dashes allowed. Italics sparingly. No exclamation marks.
Honesty"Medium confidence · 2 sources" beats false certainty.
NeverGamification, urgency, emoji, sales CTAs, "we" as the product.
ES · A seguir
"Kavak acaba de abrir tu puesto."
"Vale la pena: con matices."

'Competitivo' está siendo muy amplio acá. El mercado para Head of Ops en una fintech mexicana en Serie C con tu alcance: $1,900,000–2,400,000 MXN + 0.1–0.25% equity.

TonoFranco, sobrio, informado. Le habla de tú a tú a un Director.
DicciónFrases cortas. Sin guiones largos (—). Punto y coma, dos puntos o comas para introducir modificadores. Cursivas con moderación. Sin signos de exclamación.
Honestidad"Confianza media; 2 fuentes" supera la falsa certeza.

17Bilingual

Labra is LatAm-focused. The system ships bilingual from day one — English (en) and neutral LatAm Spanish (es, tú form). Both versions are produced together and stored together. vos variants (es-AR, es-UY, es-CR) are deferred for v1.

17.1 · Punctuation by language

The structural rule that makes Spanish editorial copy work without em-dashes.

EN
do
"Worth your time — with caveats."

Em-dashes allowed. Existing §16 dicción rules apply.

ES
ban

"Kavak acaba de abrir tu puesto; Director de Operaciones, CDMX."

Sin guiones largos. Punto y coma, dos puntos o comas para el modificador.

17.3 · Date formatting

EN — compact Thursday May 14 Thu · May 14 Weekday and month capitalized. No comma between weekday and day-of-month. Day-of-month cardinal.
ES — compact jueves 14 de mayo jue · 14 de mayo Weekday and month lowercase (RAE convention). No comma between weekday and day. Cardinal 14 — never 14º.
EN — relative (Tracker) 2d · 1w · 3mo · 1y Lowercase, no space. Cap at 1y; past that, switch to absolute compact form.
ES — relative (Tracker) 2d · 1sem · 3m · 1a Localized: d días, sem semana, m meses, a año. Same cap rule as EN.
EN — time 2:30pm · 8:00am 12-hour with am / pm lowercase, no space.
ES — time 14:30 · 08:00 24-hour, zero-padded. Standard LatAm convention.
Year — omit in compact contexts (masthead, kicker timestamps). Include only when temporal disambiguation is required.
Tracker timestamps — relative form (above) for everything within the past year; absolute compact form past that.
Verdict received-on — full absolute form including time. EN: Thursday May 14 · 2:30pm. ES: jueves 14 de mayo · 14:30.

17.4 · Default locale detection

For users who have not explicitly chosen a locale. This is a product-positioning decision as much as a technical one — English-default is the safe choice for global products; for LatAm-focused products, it's the wrong default.

1 Explicit user choice always wins. If the user has set a locale in onboarding, Settings, or via the global nav toggle, that choice is honored regardless of any other signal.
2 Else: browser locale (navigator.language / Accept-Language). Any en-* → English UI. Any es-* → Spanish UI (neutral LatAm tú form, regardless of country code).
3 Else: Spanish default. For any other locale (Portuguese, French, etc.), default to Spanish, not English. A Brazilian Portuguese speaker — likely passable in Spanish, rarely English-first — gets a better first impression in Spanish.

17.5 · Locale toggle in global navigation

Lives in main / global navigation — not buried in Settings. Accessible from every surface. Two-state in v1; structure anticipates a three-state or grouped control when vos variants ship without over-engineering it now.

v1 — two-state
v1 — ES active
narrow viewport (<640px)
tap to cycle
future — grouped (vos)
Pattern — reuses the existing .mode-toggle segmented pill. Identical chrome, locale-specific labels.
Labels — uppercase 2-letter codes (EN / ES) in Geist Mono. Universal; no localization of the toggle itself.
Narrow viewport — below 640px, collapse to a single chip showing the current locale with a ⇄ glyph. Tap cycles to the next locale (instant switch, no menu).

17.6 · Locale switch behavior

Instant. No loading state.

When a user toggles locale, the change is immediate. No re-fetch, no re-generation, no spinner. Both language versions of every LLM-generated field live in the same payload ({ en, es }); switching is a render-time concern.

{ pick: { headline: { en: "Kavak just opened your seat.", es: "Kavak acaba de abrir tu puesto." }, verdict_shape: "pursue", // internal code stays EN → "Avanza" in ES UI received_at: "2026-05-12T14:30:00Z" } }

Design implication. The toggle UX reflects the instant behavior. No spinner, no transition, no "loading…" — the page just re-renders in the new language.

17.7 · Input ≠ output language

A cross-cutting rule for every surface that consumes user-provided input (Verdict, Brief, Tracker outreach). User-facing system output always follows the user's locale, regardless of source-material language.

Input · EN
Pasted recruiter DM

"Hi — open Head of Operations role at Kavak, competitive comp, Series C. Want to chat?"

Output · ES (user locale)
Verdict commentary

"El recruiter cita 'competitive comp' en su DM; vale la pena pedir el rango. El mercado para Head of Operations en una fintech mexicana en Serie C: $1.9–2.4M MXN."

Entity names — stay in source language as proper nouns. "Head of Operations" is not translated to "Jefe de Operaciones" if that's the actual role title in the source. Company names, recruiter names, real role titles preserve source form.
Direct quotes — may preserve source language for fidelity (the 'competitive comp' above).
System commentary — around the quote follows user locale.
LLM prompt — must include user locale and an explicit "produce in this locale regardless of source" instruction. Without it, LLMs mirror the input language. The bilingual single-call generation pattern makes enforcement automatic — both versions exist, user locale picks at render.

17.8 · Kicker localization

The kicker pattern is used wherever a category or context label sits above a content unit. Visual treatment (Geist Mono, uppercase, letter-spaced, ink-3) does not change between languages. Content rules:

SUBJECT
YOUR FRESHNESS  ·  TU FRESHNESS
SUBJECT
THE PICK  ·  LA SELECCIÓN
CATEGORY
№ 1 · STRONG FIT · 2D · LINKEDIN LATAM  ·  № 1 · BUEN AJUSTE · 2D · LINKEDIN LATAM
CONTEXT
YOUR PIPELINE · MERCADO LIBRE · 7D  ·  TU PIPELINE · MERCADO LIBRE · 7D
Subject prefixes — YOUR → TU, THE → LA / EL depending on noun gender.
Category labels — localized via the i18n catalog.
Entity context — proper nouns stay in source language (Mercado Libre, LinkedIn LatAm).
Timeframes — universal single-letter abbreviations work in both (D · W · M · Y).
Overflow rule — ES kickers can run ~30% longer than their EN twins (TU PIPELINE · siguiente vs YOUR PIPELINE · next). When width exceeds the slot, auto-shrink the font from 10.5px down to 9.5px (min) and proportionally reduce tracking from +1.2 to +0.9. Never wrap; never truncate — the kicker is one line, always.

17.9 · Architectural summary

a Bilingual at generation. All text / body / headline / summary fields on user-facing payloads are { en, es } objects. No async translation flow.
b Internal codes stay English. DB values, API values, analytics, logs, cross-team comms. UI labels resolve via i18n catalog at render. Avoids translation-consistency risk.
c i18n catalog for static strings. All UI strings (buttons, labels, empty states, errors) live in one catalog covering both locales. Locale state lives in client session, broadcast to all components — no per-component fetching.
d vos is deferred. Real and important, but launching with es-AR / es-UY / es-CR variants doubles voice authoring without proven need. Toggle structure anticipates the third state.

17.10 · Numbers, currency, quotes

Per-language formatting conventions for long-form numerics and editorial punctuation. Compact display tokens (Geist Mono receipts) remain universal; these rules apply where numbers and quotes appear in prose.

EN
Currency · long-form
$1,900,000.00 USD  ·  $115,000 USD
Numbers in copy

"1,000 mutuals at Kavak; 3 in your second-degree."

Quote marks

Curly " " for direct quotes. Straight ' ' for inline single-word emphasis (e.g. 'competitive').

Questions

Closing ? only. No opening mark.

ES
Moneda · forma larga
$1,900,000.00 MXN  ·  $115,000 USD

Mexican convention (coma para miles, punto para decimales). Match locale of currency, not user.

Números en texto

"1,000 contactos en Kavak; 3 en segundo grado."

Comillas

Rectas " " para citas. Comillas simples rectas ' ' para énfasis (p. ej. 'competitivo'). Sin guillemets « ».

Preguntas

Apertura ¿ obligatoria (RAE). "¿Vale la pena el rango?", nunca "Vale la pena el rango?".

Currency rule — number format matches the currency's locale, not the user's. MXN and USD both follow US convention (1,000.00) regardless of UI language. Other LatAm currencies will follow their local convention when added.
Compact tokens unchanged — MXN $1.9M, USD $115K, $1.9–2.4M stay universal across both languages.
Exclamation — banned in both languages (per §16). ¡ doesn't come up.
